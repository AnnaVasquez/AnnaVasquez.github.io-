Anna Vasquez Hernandez

Education Major 🍎

My professional experience so far has focused on supporting students in their education through volunteer work. As an Education major at the University of California, Riverside (UCR), I am interested in becoming an elementary school teacher and helping young students develop the knowledge and skills they need to succeed. Although I am still beginning my professional journey, my experiences volunteering at an elementary school as a mentor and currently volunteering as an online tutor have allowed me to explore my interests in education and gain experience working with students.

One of my first experiences was volunteering at an elementary school as a student mentor. In this role, I had the opportunity to support elementary school students and contribute to their learning and personal development. Being a mentor allowed me to understand the importance of providing students with encouragement, guidance, and support. Students learn at different paces, and some may need additional help to understand lessons or feel confident in their abilities. This experience helped me recognize how important it is for students to have someone who is willing to listen to them, be patient with them, and encourage them to keep trying. It also strengthened my interest in working with younger students and creating a positive learning environment where they feel comfortable asking questions and learning from their mistakes.

Currently, I volunteer as an online tutor for students who need extra academic support. Through this experience, I have the opportunity to help students with their learning outside of a traditional classroom setting. My goal is to help students better understand their schoolwork and provide additional support when they are struggling with certain subjects or assignments. Tutoring has also helped me recognize the importance of being patient and understanding that every student has different needs. Some students may need an explanation more than once or benefit from approaching a problem in a different way. As a future educator, I believe it is important to be flexible and find ways to support students based on how they learn best. Both of these volunteer experiences have contributed to my personal and professional growth. They have helped me better understand the responsibilities involved in supporting students and the positive impact that educators and mentors can have on their lives. I have also learned that education involves more than teaching academic subjects. It includes encouraging students, building their confidence, and helping them feel supported throughout their learning journey. These experiences have motivated me to continue developing my communication, patience, and problem-solving skills.
